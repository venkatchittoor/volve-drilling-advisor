### 1. Executive Summary

Well 15/9-F-15 penetrated the Draupne Formation, the full Hugin Reservoir interval (3,350–3,950 m), and approximately 135 m of sub-Hugin stratigraphy to TD at 4,085 m. Drilling performance was acceptable throughout, with ROP stabilising in the 29–32 m/hr range across the main reservoir interval and MSE proxies consistently indicating efficient bit operation. The primary technical concern is a persistent **formation classification mismatch** across the 3,795–3,950 m interval, where real-time logs flagged SHALE/POOR quality despite offset well data and resistivity analogs confirming this zone as the highest-confidence HC-bearing sand in the well. Additionally, a **sub-Hugin geological anomaly at 4,000–4,050 m** — characterised by shale lithology where offset wells predict HC sand — requires immediate geomechanical and structural review before completion planning is finalised.

### 3. Drilling Performance Summary

**Overall Assessment: Mechanically Sound — Geologically Complex**

ROP performance improved markedly with depth, recovering from a sluggish upper Hugin entry (~16–18 m/hr at 3,325–3,485 m) to a sustained plateau of **29–32 m/hr** across the main reservoir and sub-Hugin interval (3,595–4,055 m). This ROP improvement coincided with decreasing MSE proxies, suggesting improved bit-rock interaction rather than a change in applied parameters.

**Notable Mechanical Concerns:**
- **RPM consistently critically low** (1.46–2.0 across multiple windows). This requires verification as to whether readings reflect surface RPM, downhole MWD RPM, or a sensor calibration issue. If accurate, low RPM at these depths in a deviated wellbore carries stick-slip and BHA fatigue risk.
- **WOB variability was high** at upper Hugin entry (σ = 28,114 N at 3,325 m), moderating downhole. High WOB σ/μ ratios (up to 0.41 at 3,645 m) indicate intermittent weight transfer likely associated with wellbore tortuosity or formation hardness contrasts.
- **ROP drop flag triggered** at 3,375 m and 3,485 m, consistent with entry into tight cemented Hugin sandstone. No drop flags triggered below 3,500 m.

### 6. Anomalies & Deviations from Offset Prognosis

**1. Persistent SHALE Classification in Confirmed HC Reservoir (3,795–3,950 m)**
The most significant deviation. VSH mean of 0.83 in what offset wells confirm as clean HC-bearing sand (GR ~14–44 API, Rt >900 Ω·m) is physically inconsistent and represents either a tool standoff issue, borehole rugosity effect on neutron/density readings, or a data processing artefact in the real-time classification algorithm. This is not a geological interpretation — it is a data quality flag.

**2. Upper Hugin Tighter Than Prognosis (3,350–3,600 m)**
PHIF values of 0.035–0.101 in the upper Hugin are below offset expectations. The cemented matrix and water saturation (SW ≈ 1.0) across this interval suggest either a diagenetic overprint not resolved in offset analogs or a lateral variation in cement volume. This materially reduces the completion target window in the upper section.

**3. Sub-Hugin Anomaly at 4,000–4,050 m Not Predicted**
Offset data predicted HC-bearing sand at this depth. The well encountered GR 74–110 API shale with RHOB 2.43–2.50 g/cc — a significant lithological departure. Possible causes include: (a) fault-related displacement of the sand body, (b) lateral pinch-out of the sub-Hugin sand facies, or (c) wellbore trajectory exiting the target zone. This deviation has material implications for any planned appraisal or infill well in this structural position.

**4. RPM Anomaly Throughout**
Surface or MWD RPM readings of 1.46–2.0 across a 700+ m interval are mechanically implausible as true rotary values during rotary drilling. This is likely a logging/sensor issue but must be confirmed against original WITS/EDR data. If genuine, it would indicate a severely underperforming surface drive system throughout the reservoir section.
